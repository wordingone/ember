6. The repeat pattern

  1. Gate D has never been met. 12 readings, range 0.222–0.285, floor 0.2857 [§3 D]. Two findings bound it: - The best score a letter-prior model can reach equals the floor exactly (0.28571), and the bar is a strict "above". So D can only pass on genuine item or image signal of about 17 items out of 847 [seat record: issue1945-final-run-prediction-20260924.md]. - ~~The image rarely moves the answer.~~ VOID (erratum 1): every missing/wrong control from A8 to A14 ran an evaluator that ignored the image condition, so none of those numbers measures the image. The first valid control (2026-09-27, A14 child) gave real 0.263, missing 0.283, wrong 0.253: the image does not help on MMMU. - The four-hour v3 trajectory (0.263, 0.283, 0.285, 0.263) was ruled a random walk, not learning [ledger 13981].
  2. Fixing one gate broke another. A7 and A9 cleared A and B with D as the only failing gate. The switch to v3 made to fix D dropped A and B to 58–62k while D stayed unmet [ledger]. A7–A9 had A/B but not D; A12–A14 had C but lost A/B. No attempt has held A, B and D together. The rows show the correlation but do not diagnose why v3 throughput fell by about 25–30%.
  3. Dispatching an attempt already known to fail a gate [×4]. A11–A14 all went out with at least one known-failing gate, using the "cannot be ACCEPTED" clause. That clause is now repealed [rules/1945-no-hour…].
  4. FP8 was retried about 17 times. It produced one small isolated win (−3.3%) and many governed regressions [§3 A].
  5. Isolated or eager measurements that did not carry over to the governed path [×7 or more]. Three isolated-probe overpredictions [GH c5781142937]. 76 rows based on an eager trace [registry].
